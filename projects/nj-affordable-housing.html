NJ Affordable Housing

Do New Jersey's affordable housing obligations lead towns to permit more housing, and which towns are most likely to fall short?

python · geospatial · gradient boosting

The question

New Jersey's Mount Laurel doctrine requires every municipality to plan for its fair share of affordable housing, and published studies disagree on whether it produces more homes. The project asks what towns have measurably done under their obligations, and whether the factors behind that record can flag which towns are most at risk of falling short in the 2025 to 2035 round.

Where it stands

The research plan is drafted. A check of the state's municipal housing reports found that the original outcome, completed affordable units per town, cannot yet be built reliably from public data. The question is being re-scoped before any modeling starts; the leading option compares multifamily permits in towns with and without court-approved plans.

How it will be tested

A simple baseline comes first: the statewide average for every town, then a three-variable logistic model. A more complex model has to beat both on whole counties held out from training, and its probabilities are checked for calibration. Feature importance is reported as association, not cause. Where possible, predictions are scored against the state's annual housing reports as they are released.
