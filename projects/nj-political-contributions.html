○ Idea

NJ Political Contributions

Do a company's past political contributions in New Jersey predict the public contracts it wins later, beyond what its contract history already predicts?

Python · record linkage · gradient boosting

The question

Among businesses that file New Jersey pay-to-play disclosures, do past contributions improve forecasts of future contract value over a model that uses contract history alone? And did the 2023 Elections Transparency Act, which raised contribution limits and replaced local pay-to-play rules, change that relationship?

This is a prediction question, not a finding of wrongdoing. Firms that already win government work may also be more politically active, for entirely legal reasons.

Where it stands

The research plan is drafted. The state's disclosure files have been reviewed: they cover 2006 to 2025, include municipal, county and school contracts, and use vendor IDs that persist across years, which makes linking records far easier. Nothing is built yet.

How it will be tested

Two baselines come first: next year's contracts equal this year's, and a model that uses contract history alone. Contribution data counts only if it improves forecasts on later years held out from training, with an interval on the improvement. A test enforces that no training row uses information from the year being predicted.

Company records are matched with probabilistic record linkage, checked against hand-labeled pairs. Published findings will be aggregate only, with no claims about named firms.
