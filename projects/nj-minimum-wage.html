NJ Minimum Wage

What did New Jersey's minimum wage increases, beginning in July 2019, do to employment and wages at fast-food restaurants?

python · causal inference · labor data

The question

Did New Jersey's minimum wage increases from July 2019 onward change employment, average weekly wages or the number of establishments at limited-service restaurants? The state raised its minimum from $8.85 in 2019 to roughly $15 by 2024, while neighboring Pennsylvania has stayed at $7.25 since 2009. It is the same border comparison behind Card and Krueger's well-known 1994 study, now with a much larger increase.

Where it stands

The research plan is drafted, and the main data source, the federal Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, has been checked. Nothing is built yet. The analysis plan will be written and dated before any post-2019 results are examined.

How it will be tested

Simple methods come first: a before-and-after comparison with Pennsylvania, a difference-in-differences model and an event study. More advanced methods, such as synthetic control, have to agree with them or explain why not. Placebo tests use a false treatment date, untreated states and an industry with few minimum wage workers, and each should show no effect.

As a calibration check, the same pipeline must reproduce published estimates for California's 2024 fast-food wage law before any New Jersey number is trusted. Results are reported with confidence intervals, including “no detectable effect” if that is what the data show.
