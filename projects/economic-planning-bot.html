Idea

Economic Planning Bot

A computational economic planner, built in phases from an input-output model on real US data to a central planner that coordinates smaller supply-chain and forecasting bots.

Status
Idea
Started
November 10, 2026
Team
Mark and Aaron Scott
Tools
Python, Linear programming, Simulation

The question

Under what information and incentive conditions can a computational planner match or beat a market at allocating resources, judged on welfare, shortages, waste and recovery from shocks?

Where it stands

  • Scope, success criteria and the phase plan are decided. The first phase is a static input-output planner on US government (BEA) input-output tables.

How we're approaching it

  • Phase A: a static input-output planner on real US data, with supply-shock scenarios.
  • Phase B: a supply-chain bot, starting from the classic beer distribution game.
  • Phase C: a simulated economy where a planner and a market run on the same world. This is the main test.
  • Phase D: a forecaster that predicts demand and commodity trends for the other layers.

A negative or mixed result still counts as a result. What would not count is a planner that wins only because the simulation handed it perfect information.

Why it matters

  • Planning versus markets is a long-running debate in economics. A careful, reproducible computational test of it is unusual.
  • It is a deep practice ground for optimization at scale, input-output economics, agent-based simulation and reinforcement learning.
  • The supply-chain and commodity-chain layers solve real business problems on their own.
